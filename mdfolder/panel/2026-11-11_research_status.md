## 分野の教授（view:professor (opus)）

Strongest objection first: in every model trained by self-play that contains R blocks, the attention never trained. This covers the dobutsu RRTRRT and 1R5T and all 9 R-containing v2 configs, and in the 9x9 paper R3(RRT) T blocks 7 and 10 never trained and block 4 only started after 20k steps. Their query-key weights follow the pure weight-decay curve at every checkpoint in 3 runs. So "nearly uniform attention" in those models says nothing about which squares the model attends to. It shows that the module was not used, and the normalized 9x9 maps show only the small differences left from the random initialization. The second objection is that the project measures plausibility (are attention weights large on squares that the complete analysis marks as important), while the stated aim ("what the model thinks") needs evidence that the model actually uses those squares, and the one causal test does not support reading the head as the reason for the move. Replacing 6T b5h4 with uniform weights costs the policy only 0.4 points of top-1 (best-vs-other −0.001) but costs the value 2.9 points of sign accuracy. In the paper's 6T, query-key size does not predict alignment with good moves (Spearman 0.02). So the one positive result is one value-related head in one run, and its headline 90.1% has no specificity control. Novelty against the literature is small as the paper presents it. Two earlier works already compared trained networks with perfect-play data: Nakayashiki & Kaneko (GPW 2019) measured AlphaZero against Tanaka's dobutsu analysis, and Haque, Wei & Müller (ACG 2021) measured Leela against chess tablebases. Neither is cited. The debate on attention as explanation (Jain & Wallace; Wiegreffe & Pinter; Abnar & Zuidema) and Jenner et al. (2024) were in the 10-08 draft but were dropped from the submitted paper. The 9x9 half is uninformative for three reasons at once: wrong value targets, attention that was never used, and 3 positions from 1 game. What would make this a strong contribution is already half-measured in the repository. (1) A mechanism, and a cheap check that others can reuse, for why attention in hybrid conv+Transformer AlphaZero networks stays unused under self-play while supervised learning trains some heads. ResTNet's own 19x19 Go R3(RRT), whose attention maps showed concepts, was trained by supervised learning on Tygem games. (2) Using the solved game to separate plausibility from faithfulness, with interventions (patching, probes) on the heads that are actually used. (3) Ranking attribution methods and baselines with the solved game, where exact Shapley values over pieces can be computed. ## 査読者（view:reviewer (claude-fable-5-2)）

What the submitted paper really shows is narrow. In one training run of the all-Transformer network (6T) on dobutsu shogi, one attention head (T block 5, head 4) puts its largest weight, seen from the mover's lion, on the opponent lion when that lion can be captured (90.1%) and almost never when it cannot (3.1%, my check today), and on a best lion destination in 79.9% of positions where no lion can be captured. At a journal or an ACG/CoG-level conference I would recommend rejection in the present form and require: (1) several independent training runs with a head-selection rule fixed in advance; (2) causal tests of heads, not only agreement counts; (3) a 9x9 study on a correctly trained model with thousands of positions and an engine reference; (4) a strength measure that works (for dobutsu, play against the complete analysis); (5) an IG baseline chosen by tests against ground truth; (6) an explanation, with an intervention, of why attention stays unused in networks with R blocks.
